Skunk and Strains: What You Should Know About the Green Cannabis

A deep dive into skunk (cannabis flower), the difference between Indica and Sativa, and how different terpenes affect the citizen's experience.

SmokingTracker Team

In substance abuse treatment, we often meet citizens who specifically choose skunk (also called flower, weed, or bud) over hashish. For outsiders, it might all seem like the same substance, but the reality is significantly different, especially regarding the experience, potency, and patterns of habituation.

Perhaps the biggest advantage of skunk, as highlighted by many citizens, is the ability to smoke it "completely clean" (all green) without mixing it with tobacco. This eliminates the nicotine addiction, which often keeps citizens trapped in substance abuse long after they actually want to quit cannabis.

That being said, skunk today is not the same low-potency product as in the 70s. Modern cultivation techniques have created a broad spectrum of extremely specialized and potent plants.

Here is what you, as a counselor, should know about the "green gold."

The Difference Between Hash and Skunk

While hash is heated and pressed resin (trichomes) from the plant, skunk is simply the dried and cured female flowers (buds).

Because the plant material is still intact, skunk burns differently and retains a much more complex profile of terpenes — the aromatic oils that give cannabis its smell (e.g., pine needle, citrus, earth, or wild berries).

It is a widespread misconception that "skunk" is automatically stronger than hash. A clean, high-quality hash will almost always have a higher percentage of THC (the active ingredient) per gram than the flower itself, because the hash is a concentrate.

Nevertheless, the citizen may experience the high from skunk as more "intense." This is due to several factors:

  1. Skunk is often smoked clean (without tobacco), which means a whole joint can contain more cannabis overall if the citizen is not paying attention to the scale-up in dosage.
  2. The intact terpene profile in the flower creates a so-called "entourage effect," which can amplify the psychotropic effects of the THC.

Indica vs. Sativa (And why it's almost a myth today)

When citizens talk about what type of skunk they smoke, they almost always refer to two overarching categories: Indica and Sativa.

  • Sativa-dominant strains: Known for providing a "head high" — a more energetic, uplifting, creative, and stimulating experience. Citizens often use it during the day.
  • Indica-dominant strains: Known for providing a "body stone" — a heavy, relaxing, sedative, and pain-relieving experience. ("Indica = In da couch"). Citizens often use it in the evening to sleep.

Clinical insight: Modern science shows, however, that the classification (botanically speaking) tells us very little about the high. Almost all modern cannabis is hybrids (crossbreeds). The crucial factor is not whether the plant is broad- or narrow-leaved (Indica/Sativa), but rather its chemical profile, specifically the terpene composition.

Terpenes like Myrcene (found in hops/mangoes) have a strongly sedative effect and are often found in "indica," while Limonene (found in citrus) is uplifting and often dominates "sativa" strains.

Understand Your Citizen's Choice

In the modern cannabis environment, practically all skunk is named after its exact lineage ("strain"), e.g., Sour Diesel, Granddaddy Purple, or Amnesia Haze. This is far more widespread than with hash.

If you want to understand why a citizen smokes the exact type they do, it can be enormously rewarding to look up their preferred strain. Websites like Leafly.com function as a complete encyclopedia of cannabis strains.

When the citizen tells you they smoke Blue Dream, you can look it up and see: "Ah, Leafly says it primarily helps with stress and depression, but can cause dry mouth and anxiety in large quantities." This provides an entirely new starting point for the therapeutic conversation about self-medication.
